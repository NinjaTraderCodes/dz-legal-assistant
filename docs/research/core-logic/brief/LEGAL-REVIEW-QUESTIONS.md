# Legal Review questions (batch 1, 11 November 2026)

A copy of the owner's Legal Review page, in English (the quotes stay in Arabic), for agents that cannot open the page. Each question shows what the app does now, the question, and what the answer changes.

## A. The «في الشكل» paragraph and the request to admit the claim in form

*Applied in the app on 11 November 2026: the answer confirms or corrects it.*

### Q01. Wording of the «في الشكل» paragraph and the articles it cites (high priority)

**What the app does now.** In every petition whose mandatory elements are complete, the app writes:

> حيث إن هذه العريضة جاءت مستوفية للشروط المنصوص عليها قانونا، سيما المواد 13 و14 و15 و17 من قانون الإجراءات المدنية والإدارية، مما يتعين معه قبولها شكلا.

(“Whereas this petition meets the conditions laid down by law, in particular CPCA articles 13, 14, 15 and 17, so that it must be admitted in form.”) It adds article 426 when the claim is before the family section.

**The question.** Is this wording sound, and are these the right articles? Article 13 is about standing and interest; article 17 is about paying the fees (para. 1) and publishing a real-estate petition (para. 3), even when the claim is not about real estate; article 426 is about territorial jurisdiction in family cases.

**What the answer changes.** We correct the fixed text that appears in every petition the app produces.

**Options offered:** (1) The wording and the articles are right as they are. (2) The wording is right, but the articles change (write which to remove or add). (3) The paragraph is reworded (write the suggested wording). (4) This paragraph should not be in the petition at all.

### Q02. What goes in place of «في الشكل» when a mandatory element is missing? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** Since 11 November, when a mandatory element is missing (for example the defendant's domicile), the app no longer writes that the petition “meets the conditions”. It writes in place of the paragraph:

> (تُكتب هذه الفقرة متى اكتملت البيانات الإلزامية للعريضة.)

(“This paragraph is written once the petition's mandatory elements are complete.”) The usual paragraph comes back on its own once the element is filled in.

**The question.** Is this the right behaviour?

**What the answer changes.** We keep the current behaviour or change it.

**Options offered:** (1) Right as it is. (2) Remove the paragraph entirely until the element is complete. (3) Write the paragraph and name the missing element. (4) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q03. Shape of the requests: «في الشكل» then «في الموضوع» (medium priority)

**What the app does now.** The requests section always opens like this, then the requests follow, numbered:

> في الشكل: قبول الدعوى شكلا. / في الموضوع: / 1. ...

(“In form: admit the claim in form. On the merits: 1. …”)

**The question.** Is this the usual shape before Algerian courts? Is there a line usually added to every petition (for example «تحميل المدعى عليه المصاريف القضائية», costs against the defendant)?

**What the answer changes.** We correct the fixed model of the requests section.

**Options offered:** (1) Right as it is. (2) Right, with a fixed line added (write it in the box). (3) Change the shape (write the model in the box).

## B. Mandatory elements and blocking the export

*Applied in the app on 11 November 2026: the answer confirms or corrects it.*

### Q04. Does a missing date or signature make the petition inadmissible in form? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** The app treats the petition's date and signature like the elements of article 15: if either is missing, the document can only be exported marked «مسودة» (draft). CPCA article 14:

> ترفع الدعوى أمام المحكمة بعريضة مكتوبة، موقعة ومؤرخة، تودع بأمانة الضبط من قبل المدعي أو وكيله أو محاميه، بعدد من النسخ يساوي عدد الأطراف.

Article 14 states no sanction, unlike article 15, which says «تحت طائلة عدم قبولها شكلا» (on pain of inadmissibility in form).

**The question.** In practice, does a missing date or signature make the petition inadmissible in form, or is it corrected without sanction?

**What the answer changes.** If there is no sanction, a missing date or signature becomes a warning and no longer blocks the final export.

**Options offered:** (1) Both, on pain of inadmissibility in form. (2) The signature only. (3) The date only. (4) Neither: it is corrected without sanction. (5) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q05. Legal person: is the representative's name required? (medium priority)

**What the app does now.** CPCA article 15, item 4:

> الإشارة إلى تسمية وطبيعة الشخص المعنوي، ومقره الاجتماعي وصفة ممثله القانوني أو الاتفاقي

(“The name and nature of the legal person, its registered office and the capacity of its legal or contractual representative.”) The app asks for the name, nature, registered office and the representative's capacity (for example «المسير», manager), but not the representative's name or the trade register number.

**The question.** Is the capacity enough, or do courts also require the representative's name? Is the trade register number, or anything else, required?

**What the answer changes.** We add the item to the form, as mandatory or optional depending on the answer.

**Options offered:** (1) The capacity is enough. (2) The name is also required, on pain of inadmissibility. (3) The name is advisable but not required. (4) Something else (for example the trade register number; explain in the box).

### Q06. Facts, requests and grounds: must a legal basis be cited? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** CPCA article 15, item 5: «عرضا موجزا للوقائع والطلبات والوسائل التي تؤسس عليها الدعوى» (“a brief statement of the facts, the requests and the grounds on which the claim rests”). The app treats each as a separate element: if the facts, the requests or the legal grounds section is empty, it blocks the final export.

**The question.** Is a petition that states the facts and the requests but cites no legal article inadmissible in form? Or are the “grounds” satisfied by stating the factual reasons without article numbers?

**What the answer changes.** If the grounds need no articles, the legal grounds section stays mandatory but the app accepts it without article numbers.

**Options offered:** (1) All three are mandatory, and the grounds must cite a legal basis. (2) All three are mandatory, but the grounds need not cite articles. (3) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q07. Export with a missing element: compulsory draft mark, or the lawyer's choice? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** Since 11 November, when a mandatory element is missing, the app exports the document (Word or PDF) only with the word «مسودة» (draft) visible on the pages. It cannot be exported clean until the element is completed.

**The question.** Are there cases where it is right to file a petition missing one of the elements the app checks (the list: court, plaintiff's details, defendant's details, legal person's details, facts, requests, grounds, date, signature, publication of a real-estate petition, Arabic language)? If so, should a clean export be allowed after the lawyer explicitly confirms?

**What the answer changes.** We keep the block as it is, or allow a clean export after an explicit, recorded confirmation.

**Options offered:** (1) The block is right: a petition missing one of these is never filed. (2) Allow a clean export after the lawyer explicitly confirms. (3) Something else (list the cases in the box).

## C. Article texts in the legal grounds

*Applied in the app on 11 November 2026: the answer confirms or corrects it.*

### Q08. Quoting the article verbatim, and the phrase «تنص على أنه» (high priority)

**What the app does now.** Each paragraph used to open with a summary of the article written by the engineering team («تقضي بـ...», “provides for …”), and one summary turned out to contradict the article. Since 11 November it opens with the article's own text, for example:

> حيث إن المادة 119 من القانون المدني تنص على أنه: «في العقود الملزمة للجانبين، إذا لم يوف أحد المتعاقدين بالتزامه جاز للمتعاقد الآخر بعد اعذاره المدين أن يطالب بتنفيذ العقد أو فسخه، مع التعويض في الحالتين إذا اقتضى الحال ذلك. ويجوز للقاضي أن يمنح المدين أجلا حسب الظروف، كما يجوز له أن يرفض الفسخ إذا كان ما لم يوف به المدين قليل الأهمية بالنسبة إلى كامل الالتزامات»، وحيث إن ...

Then it applies the article to the facts.

**The question.** Is quoting the article verbatim acceptable in a petition? What is the usual phrase to introduce it?

**What the answer changes.** We set the fixed phrase that opens each paragraph of the legal grounds.

**Options offered:** (1) Acceptable, and «تنص على أنه:» is right. (2) Acceptable, but use «تنص على ما يلي:». (3) Acceptable with another phrase (write it in the box). (4) Better a summary written and checked by a lawyer than the verbatim text.

### Q09. Length of the quotation: the whole article or only the relevant sentence? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** A short article (up to about 450 characters) is quoted whole, all its paragraphs. A longer one is quoted by its first paragraph only, cut at a comma and marked «…» if still long. Example: in a claim for goods not delivered, if Civil Code articles 119 and 367 are ticked, both are quoted whole: about 700 characters before they are applied to the facts.

**The question.** Is this length right, or should only the sentence relevant to the claim be quoted?

**What the answer changes.** If the answer is “only the relevant sentence”, a lawyer marks for each catalogue article which sentence to quote (added to the catalogue review).

**Options offered:** (1) Quoting short articles whole is right. (2) Quote only the part relevant to the claim. (3) Cite the article number without quoting, then apply it. (4) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q10. Taṭlīq: should the specific ground the wife relies on be quoted? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** Family Code article 53 lists ten grounds for taṭlīq. The app does not know which ground the claim rests on, so it quotes only the article's opening:

> حيث إن المادة 53 من قانون الأسرة تنص على أنه: «يجوز للزوجة أن تطلب التطليق للأسباب الآتية» …

(“The wife may request taṭlīq on the following grounds …”)

**The question.** Should the relevant ground be quoted after the opening (for example «3 – الهجر في المضجع فوق أربعة أشهر», desertion of the marital bed for over four months)? Can one claim rest on more than one ground?

**What the answer changes.** If yes, the app asks which ground (or grounds) and quotes it after the opening.

**Options offered:** (1) Yes, quote the relevant ground or grounds after the opening. (2) The opening is enough; the ground is stated in the facts and the argument. (3) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q11. Abusive dismissal: article 73 or article 73-4? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** In the app's catalogue, the basis of an abusive-dismissal claim is article 73 of Law 90-11, so it quotes:

> يتم التسريح التأديبي في حالة ارتكاب العامل أخطاء جسيمة.

(“Disciplinary dismissal takes place when the worker commits serious misconduct.”) Article 73-4 is listed only for damages; its second paragraph reads:

> وإذا حدث تسريح العامل خرقا لأحكام المادة 73 أعلاه، يعتبر تعسفيا.

(“A dismissal in breach of article 73 is abusive.”)

**The question.** Which article (or articles) does an abusive-dismissal claim usually rest on? Are the app's texts of articles 73 and 73-4 the texts in force after the amendments to Law 90-11?

**What the answer changes.** We correct the basis articles of this claim type in the catalogue, and so the text quoted.

**Options offered:** (1) Articles 73 and 73-4 together, and both texts are current. (2) Article 73-4 is the main basis; article 73 defines serious misconduct. (3) Other articles (list them in the box).

### Q12. Taṭlīq and khulʿ: the articles on consequences (iddah, custody, housing, household goods, maintenance, guardianship) (high priority)

**What the app does now.** In the app's catalogue, the basis articles of a taṭlīq claim (besides article 53) and of a khulʿ claim (besides article 54) include these Family Code articles: 61 (iddah), 64 (custody), 72 (the custodian's housing), 73 (disputes over household goods), 75 (child maintenance), 87 (guardianship). If the lawyer ticks them, the app quotes them in the legal grounds.

**The question.** Are these articles a basis of the claim itself, or only of the accessory requests (custody, maintenance, housing, household goods)? Does article 73 (household goods) belong in a khulʿ claim?

**What the answer changes.** If they serve only the accessory requests, the app ties each article to its request (for example article 64 to the custody request) instead of quoting it as a basis of the taṭlīq or khulʿ.

**Options offered:** (1) Basis of the accessory requests only; tie each to its request. (2) Basis of the whole claim, as now. (3) Remove some of them (list them in the box). (4) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q13. Are the app's texts of amended articles the texts in force? (medium priority)

**What the app does now.** The app quotes texts taken from the official published versions. The main articles amended after their law was enacted: Commercial Code 187 مكرر (Law 05-02), Family Code 53, 54 and 72 (Ordinance 05-02), Civil Code 467 مكرر (Law 07-05), and articles 73 and 73-4 of Law 90-11.

**The question.** Has any of these articles, or any article in the claim reference at the bottom of the page, been amended since, in a way the app has missed?

**What the answer changes.** We correct the text in the app's library, which corrects the quotation in every petition.

**Options offered:** (1) No later amendment that I know of. (2) Yes (give the article and the amending text in the box).

## D. The competent section

*Proposed correction, not applied yet.*

### Q14. Dwelling leases and eviction of an occupier: civil or real-estate section? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** The app's sample claim for unpaid rent on a dwelling is filed at the civil section («القسم المدني»). CPCA article 512:

> ينظر القسم العقاري على الخصوص في القضايا الآتية : ... 10 - في إيجار السكنات والمحلات المهنية

(“The real-estate section hears in particular: … 10 – leases of dwellings and professional premises.”) Article 32 has a misfiled case moved to the right section by the registry, so the error delays the case but does not make it inadmissible.

**The question.** In practice, which section hears a claim for unpaid rent on a dwelling or professional premises? And a claim to evict an occupier without title (Civil Code article 674)?

**What the answer changes.** We correct the sample and add a warning when the lawyer picks a section that is not the usual one for the claim type.

**Options offered:** (1) The real-estate section for both. (2) Real-estate section for the lease, civil section for the eviction. (3) The civil section for both. (4) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q15. Annulling the sale of registered land: civil or real-estate section? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** The sample «بيع أرض محل نزاع» (annulling the sale of a plot of land for fraud; the deed is registered) is filed at the civil section. CPCA article 515:

> ينظر القسم العقاري في الدعاوى المتعلقة بإبطال أو فسخ أو تعديل أو نقض الحقوق المترتبة على عقود تم شهرها.

(“The real-estate section hears claims to annul, rescind, amend or revoke rights arising from registered deeds.”)

**The question.** Is this claim filed at the real-estate section?

**What the answer changes.** We correct the sample, and propose the real-estate section for every claim about a registered deed.

**Options offered:** (1) Yes, the real-estate section. (2) The civil section. (3) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q16. Commercial claims: commercial section or specialised commercial court? (medium priority)

**What the app does now.** The app proposes the commercial section for three claim types: a commercial debt, a commercial lease, and goods not delivered. CPCA article 536 مكرر gives the specialised commercial court certain disputes (intellectual property, commercial companies, receivership and bankruptcy, banks and financial institutions against traders, maritime, air transport and insurance linked to trade, international trade), and article 536 مكرر 4 requires conciliation before the claim is registered there.

**The question.** Do these three claim types stay with the ordinary court's commercial section? When do they fall to the specialised commercial court (for example a debt between two commercial companies, or a bank's claim against a trader)?

**What the answer changes.** We add a question about the parties, and a warning about the prior conciliation when the claim belongs to the specialised court.

**Options offered:** (1) All three always go to the commercial section. (2) Some go to the specialised court depending on the parties (explain in the box). (3) Something else (explain in the box).

## E. Deadlines and prior steps

### Q17. Labour disputes: prior conciliation and the six-month deadline (high priority)

**What the app does now.** The app reminds the lawyer of the prior conciliation before the conciliation office (Law 90-04, article 19) and asks for the non-conciliation record in the facts, but it computes no deadline. CPCA article 504:

> يجب رفع الدعوى أمام القسم الاجتماعي في أجل لا يتجاوز ستة (6) أشهر من تاريخ تسليم محضر عدم الصلح، وذلك تحت طائلة سقوط الحق في رفع الدعوى.

(“The claim must be brought before the labour section within six months of the delivery of the non-conciliation record, on pain of forfeiture of the right to sue.”)

**What we propose to build.** The app asks for the date the record was delivered. If more than six months have passed at the petition's date, it shows article 504 and blocks the clean export (export only with the «مسودة» mark).

**The question.** - Is this right?
- Does the period run from the delivery of the record to the worker, or from the date it was drawn up?
- When is conciliation not required (article 19 para. 2: defendant living abroad, bankruptcy, receivership) or excluded from the conciliation offices (para. 3: civil servants and agents under the general statute of public institutions and administrations), and what does the lawyer write instead of the record?

**What the answer changes.** We build the deadline and the record requirement as proposed, or with the stated correction.

**Options offered:** (1) Right as proposed. (2) Right, with a correction to the computation or the exceptions (explain in the box). (3) A warning only, not a block. (4) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q18. Formal notice before rescission or damages (high priority)

**What the app does now.** Civil Code article 119 requires formal notice to the debtor before claiming performance or rescission, and article 179:

> لا يستحق التعويض إلا بعد اعذار المدين ما لم يوجد نص مخالف لذلك.

(“No damages are due until the debtor has been given formal notice, unless a text provides otherwise.”) Today the app only looks for the word «إعذار» in the lawyer's notes, which is unreliable: «لم يوجه أي إعذار» (“no notice was served”) contains the word and silences the warning.

**What we propose to build.** An explicit question in the form: was formal notice served? By what means? On what date? Then a warning if it was not, in claims for rescission, damages, unpaid rent, loan repayment and unpaid price.

**The question.** - Which means of formal notice are accepted in practice (a bailiff's record, a registered letter with acknowledgment of receipt, other)?
- Does a missing notice lead to the request being rejected, so the clean export should be blocked, or is a warning enough?
- In which of these claims is notice not required?

**What the answer changes.** We set the choices for the means of notice, the level of the alert (warning or block), and the claims where the question is asked.

**Options offered:** (1) A warning is enough. (2) Block the clean export in rescission and damages claims. (3) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q19. Commercial leases: before and after 2005 (medium priority)

**What the app does now.** The app asks the lawyer to state the lease date (before or after 2005) in the facts and computes no deadline. Commercial Code article 187 مكرر (Law 05-02): leases signed after the law's publication must be notarial on pain of nullity, and the tenant must leave at the end of the term «دون حاجة إلى توجيه تنبيه بالإخلاء ودون الحق في الحصول على تعويض الاستحقاق» (without any notice to quit and with no right to the eviction indemnity). Article 173 governs leases under the earlier rules:

> لا ينتهي إيجار المحلات الخاصة بهذه الأحكام إلا بأثر تنبيه بالإخلاء حسبما جرت عليه العادات المحلية وفي مدة ستة أشهر قبل الأجل على الأقل.

(“Such leases end only by a notice to quit given according to local custom at least six months before the term.”)

**What we propose to build.** For earlier leases: the app asks the date of the notice to quit and warns if it was served less than six months before the term. For later leases: it warns if the lease is not notarial.

**The question.** Is this split right? Are there other deadlines to warn about (for example the period for claiming the eviction indemnity after the notice to quit)?

**What the answer changes.** We build both warnings as proposed and add any deadlines the answer names.

**Options offered:** (1) Right as proposed. (2) Right, with other deadlines (list them in the box). (3) Something else (explain in the box).

### Q22. Limitation: warning or block? Which periods matter most? (high priority)

**What the app does now.** The app computes no limitation period, and the limitation articles (such as Civil Code 101, 133, 308, 309 and 312) are not in its library yet.

**The question.** - Is this distinction right: limitation as a warning, forfeiture as a block?

**What the answer changes.** We add those limitation articles to the app's library and build a warning per claim type from the answer.

**Options offered:** (1) The distinction is right, and the periods listed are the main ones. (2) The distinction is right, with other periods (list them in the box). (3) Something else (explain in the box).
